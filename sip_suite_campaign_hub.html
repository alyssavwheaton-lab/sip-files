3. Suite overview

The SIP suite at a glance

Three strategies. Three distinct use cases. One cleaner advisor conversation around income, quality, and portfolio role.

SIP

Strategic Income Portfolio

Core income anchor for clients seeking a balanced approach to yield, quality, and diversification. Useful when the conversation starts with dependable portfolio income and a measured risk profile.

SIP Plus

Strategic Income Portfolio Plus

Step-up income option for clients willing to accept a broader opportunity set in pursuit of higher income potential. Useful when advisors need to address cash-flow needs without defaulting to a single-sector answer.

Global SIP

Global Strategic Income Portfolio

Globally oriented income strategy for clients who may benefit from a wider set of opportunities across regions and sectors. Useful when concentration risk or relative-value dispersion becomes part of the allocation discussion.

4. Advisor client fit

How advisors can frame client fit

These are conversation starters built around portfolio role, cash-flow needs, and risk tolerance—not product-first language.

For clients prioritizing stability

Position SIP as a disciplined income anchor when the advisor needs a steadier core fixed income allocation and a credible way to discuss quality, diversification, and income durability.

For clients seeking more income

Position SIP Plus when the advisor needs a more assertive income conversation, particularly where traditional core bond exposure may feel insufficient relative to spending needs or distribution goals.

For clients needing broader diversification

Position Global SIP when the advisor wants to discuss opportunity sets beyond a domestic lens and pair income objectives with a more global relative-value framework.
